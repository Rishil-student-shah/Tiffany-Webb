Don't Bet on Your Future

Sports betting has fundamentally changed how young people consume sports. Prevention cannot rely on outdated "just say no" tactics when the danger is gamified in their pockets.

A generation ago, gambling required a physical location—a casino, a track, a corner store. Today, a sportsbook is running 24/7 in the pockets of millions of high school and college students. The normalization of sports betting, particularly through aggressively marketed apps, has blurred the line between fandom and financial risk.

The Gamification of Risk

It starts subtly. Loot boxes in video games teach children the mechanics of variable rewards early on. By the time they are old enough to watch a football game, they are inundated with "risk-free bets" and "boosted odds." It is no longer just about who wins or loses the game; it is about the micro-transactions—who scores the first touchdown, how many points a specific player scores in a quarter.

"We are fighting algorithms designed by billionaires to capture the attention—and wallets—of young people."

Starting the Conversation

Lecturing teenagers about the statistical unlikelihood of winning long-term does not work. When I conduct my "Don't Bet on Your Future" sessions at schools, I don't start by telling them gambling is bad. I start by pulling back the curtain on the industry. We talk about how the apps are engineered to keep them scrolling and betting. We talk about how "free bets" are designed to acquire their data and build habits.

When you validate their intelligence and show them how the game is rigged, they stop defending the behavior and start questioning it. That is where true youth prevention begins.
