What Gambling Prevention Actually Looks Like

There is a profound gap between launching a billboard campaign and actually shifting behavior in a community. Prevention requires more than just awareness; it requires presence.

In the public health sector, we frequently discuss "awareness." We print brochures, launch digital campaigns during Problem Gambling Awareness Month, and deploy ad spends targeting high-risk demographics. While these are necessary components of a public health strategy, they are often mistaken for the entirety of the work.

The Limitation of Broadcast Prevention

When an individual is experiencing the deep shame associated with gambling harm—watching their savings deplete, lying to their spouse, feeling the physical anxiety of the next bet—a billboard with a helpline number is rarely the catalyst for change. Shame thrives in isolation. A billboard is broadcasted; it doesn't listen.

Real prevention happens in the micro-interactions. It happens when a school counselor knows the right questions to ask a student who suddenly drops out of extracurriculars. It happens when a faith leader understands that financial struggles might not just be "tough times," but the result of an undisclosed addiction. It happens when a primary care physician includes the Brief Biosocial Gambling Screen (BBGS) in their standard intake alongside depression and alcohol screenings.

"Shame thrives in isolation. A billboard is broadcasted; it doesn't listen."

Building the Safety Net

Over my 15 years and 4,000+ hours of community outreach, I’ve learned that our job isn’t to reach every single person suffering from gambling harm. Our job is to equip the people they already trust. By training social workers, educators, and community leaders to recognize the signs, we weave a safety net directly into the fabric of the community.

If we want to move from passive awareness to active prevention, we must stop asking, "How many people saw our message?" and start asking, "Who in this community is trained to have the conversation?"
