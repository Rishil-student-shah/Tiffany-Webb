Case Studies

Real examples of how Tiffany's strategies move organizations from passive awareness to active intervention.

01
Integrating Gambling Harm into Behavioral Health Intakes
+

The Challenge: A regional health network found that while they were effectively treating substance use disorders, gambling harm was entirely undocumented and untreated. Clinicians lacked the confidence and tools to identify the intersectionality of these behaviors.

The Approach: Tiffany delivered a targeted SBIRT (Screening, Brief Intervention, and Referral to Treatment) training block over two days. She introduced the Brief Biosocial Gambling Screen (BBGS) and guided clinicians through role-play scenarios involving high-stigma populations.

The Result: Within 90 days, the network successfully integrated the 3-question screening into 100% of their intake protocols. Consequently, they identified a 15% rate of co-occurring gambling harm among their existing substance use disorder patients, allowing for immediate dual-diagnosis treatment planning.

02
Mobilizing a Community During PGAM
+

The Challenge: A local advocacy group wanted to leverage Problem Gambling Awareness Month (March) but historically struggled with low turnout and community disengagement. Their messaging was overly clinical and failed to resonate with the demographics most at risk.

The Approach: Tiffany restructured their entire outreach strategy. Utilizing her "Cultural Fluency" framework, she shifted the messaging from clinical jargon to relatable community impact. She integrated GambleFreeGear into the events to break the silence and serve as a physical conversation starter.

The Result: The organization saw a 300% increase in event attendance compared to the previous year. More importantly, the campaign resulted in 42 individuals seeking direct assistance—the highest engagement rate in the organization's history for a single awareness month.

03
Reaching the Overlooked: A Culturally Fluent Approach to Youth Outreach
+

The Challenge: A high school district noticed a sharp rise in students engaging in sports betting and loot-box gaming on campus, but traditional anti-gambling assemblies were met with apathy. The students felt they were being "talked down to."

The Approach: Tiffany deployed her "Don't Bet on Your Future" workshop. By drawing parallels between gaming mechanics, micro-transactions, and traditional betting, she validated the students' experiences. She employed peer-to-peer intervention tactics, speaking *with* them rather than *at* them.

The Result: Post-session surveys indicated that 85% of students could accurately identify predatory game mechanics. Furthermore, the district adopted Tiffany's digital hygiene framework into their core health curriculum, ensuring sustained education for future classes.
