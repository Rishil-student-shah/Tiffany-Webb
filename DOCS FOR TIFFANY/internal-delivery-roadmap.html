What the audit changed

Flag 1 — no shared foundation

Landing page and Media Kit were both starting Day 1 with no locked logo/style sheet between them — real risk of two visually inconsistent deliverables, the same failure mode we just fixed across 5 pitch-deck revision rounds.

Fix: Day 0–1 Foundation Lock, both work from one source before splitting.
Flag 2 — One-Sheet built from scratch

The One-Sheet is described in the deck as distilled FROM the Media Kit, not a standalone build. Building all three in parallel risks the one-sheet drifting from the final Media Kit content.

Fix: Sequence it 1 day after Media Kit locks, not parallel.
Flag 3 — Tiffany inputs requested late

Domain access, photos, sign-off, and especially testimonial contacts have real lag outside your control — testimonials require her contacts to reply.

Fix: Send the request list Day 0, the moment she says yes — not after Phase 1 finishes.
Flag 4 — WhatsApp needs the live booking form

CRM/booking automation logically depends on the landing page's booking form being deployed — good news, your sequencing already satisfies this since Tech finishes landing page deploy before WhatsApp starts.

No fix needed — this dependency is already correctly ordered.

Verdict

Yes — parallelize it, and yes, Tiffany will feel the benefit even though she never sees this chart: a tighter internal timeline means faster delivery against the "~6 weeks" you promised her, which is a good under-promise buffer to keep external. With the Day 0–1 Foundation Lock added, the One-Sheet resequenced, and her inputs requested immediately, this plan closes the one real risk (visual drift between the two builders) without losing any of the speed you designed it for.
